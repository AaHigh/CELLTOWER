## What I Now Understand About This Project

Having read the full `ct.zip` drop — README, vision, the April 20 status update, the stream spec, the knowledge format spec, the ode, and the live `index.html` (~375 KB, today’s build) — here is the picture as it actually stands, not as the docs aspire.

CELLTOWER is three things layered on one file. At the surface it is an NES-accurate Tetris trainer that runs from a single `index.html` on GitHub Pages, playable on iPhone Safari with no install. Beneath that it is a cryptographic score-verification engine: a SHA-256 hash chain seeded before each game, advancing on every piece lock, terminating in a receipt code — the direct descendant of your 1999 Rush 2049 work at Atari. And at the foundation it is legal-economic infrastructure: a peer-to-peer skill-wagering platform structured for California’s dominant factor test, conservatively capped, with “math as the trusted party” replacing the house. The `.ctk` knowledge format extends this into a fourth dimension — signed human gameplay as AI training data, which connects directly to your Hightower Derivative thesis. The ode makes the philosophy explicit: the corpus learns language from repetition of human decisions, and the receipts prove those decisions were human.

## The Critical Discrepancy I Found

This is the most important thing in this document. **`stream.md` declares v3.0 as “current implementation.” The code disagrees.** Today’s `index.html` (v0.6112026) still uses `_gameSeed5` — a 5-character B92 seed — at lines 3519, 7025, and throughout the stream encoder at line 7556. Per your own spec, a 5-char seed *is the v2.0 marker*; v3.0 requires a 6-char seed, seeded-PRNG piece sequence, and the 256-slot timing table. The piece sequence still comes from `Math.random()`, which means streams are not reproducible from seed, which means the verification chain has the exact weakness your own `hacker.md` Ken Thompson analysis warns about: forgeable piece selection.

The README has a parallel drift: the header says v0.6112026 but the body text still describes itself as v0.420 in three places. Small, but it’s the same disease — docs and ground truth diverging. Your spec’s own authority line (“index.html is ground truth”) means the project is currently, formally, a v2.0 system wearing v3.0 documentation.

## The Three Days

### Day 1 (Thursday) — Close the v2.0/v3.0 Gap

Bring the implementation up to the spec while you have a model that can hold the entire 375 KB file and the full stream.md in mind simultaneously. Concretely: migrate `_gameSeed5` to the 6-char seed, replace `Math.random()` piece generation with the seeded PRNG so replays are reproducible, and wire the 256-slot timing table. This is the single highest-leverage task because every downstream claim — tournament legitimacy, `.ctk` data integrity, the California legal argument — rests on reproducible verification. A v2.0 chain proves a session happened; only v3.0 proves *what* happened.

### Day 2 (Friday) — Adversarial Audit + Validator Skeleton

Run Fable as the attacker. Hand it the full verification design and ask it to forge a million-point receipt — every hole it finds now is a hole a real adversary finds after money is on the table. This is precisely the kind of red-team reasoning where the model tier matters most. Then have it scaffold the standalone validator page (item 5 on your April build order): a page that accepts a stream, replays it deterministically, and confirms the score. Even a rough validator forces the encoder to be honest, because round-tripping is the only test that counts.

### Day 3 (Saturday) — Extraction and Continuity

Spend the last day making sure nothing you and Fable built together is locked inside this model’s head. Have it write exhaustive, junior-developer-grade documentation of everything changed in days 1–2: updated stream.md reflecting *actual* implementation, a SNAPSHOTS dead-drop entry so future sessions with lesser models inherit full context, and a prioritized backlog with enough detail that Sonnet or Haiku can execute each item without re-deriving the architecture. Fix the README v0.420 ghosts while you’re in there. The Fable window ends, but a well-written spec is model-independent — that’s your own thesis applied to your own workflow: the artifact that survives is the signed, verifiable record of the thinking.

## Two Assertions Beyond the Window

First: the QR air-gap verification (build-order item 4) should wait until v3.0 is real — a QR code of a forgeable stream is theater, and you of all people know the difference between a receipt and a prop. Second: the `.ctk` corpus only becomes valuable once streams are reproducible, because annotated knowledge derived from unverifiable replays inherits the Ken Thompson taint. Fix the seed, and everything above it becomes load-bearing.
